# 10 — Jev's published accuracy vs. our local 8B (the apples-to-oranges problem)

> Fetched 2026-09-16 from https://evals.typesafe.ai/ — the accuracy numbers *do* exist, published
> alongside the launch. This doc records them, puts our local numbers next to them, and is explicit
> about why they cannot be directly compared.

## 1. What TypeSafe published

Their "workflow evals" site reports accuracy of Jev and a set of frontier models across **4 workflows**
(security incidents, agent-trace observability, invoice processing, customer service). Reference labels
are generated by **averaging GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.1 answering every question in the harness**
(both at high thinking). All other models run at provider-default reasoning.

**Jev's numbers:**

| Workflow | Accuracy | Cost/case | Time/case |
|---|---|---|---|
| Security Incidents | 61.7% | $0.0001 | 0.3 s |
| Agent Trace Observability | 71.6% | $0.0003 | 0.5 s |
| Invoice Processing | 61.8% | $0.0011 | 0.5 s |
| Customer Service | 76.0% | $0.0001 | 0.4 s |
| **Mean (equal weight)** | **67.8%** | **$0.0004** | **0.4 s** |

**Context — the same eval's other models (mean accuracy):**

| Model | Mode | Mean acc | Mean $/case | Mean time/case |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sol | workflow | 74.1% | $0.0836 | 23.3 s |
| Opus 5 | workflow | 73.1% | $0.1761 | 37.8 s |
| Terra | workflow | 67.9% | $0.0304 | 10.1 s |
| **Jev** | workflow | **67.8%** | **$0.0004** | **0.4 s** |
| Luna | workflow | 66.8% | $0.0033 | 12.9 s |
| Sonnet 5 | workflow | 67.8% | $0.1174 | 78.1 s |
| DS v4 pro | workflow | 65.5% | $0.0413 | 86.5 s |
| DS v4 flash | workflow | 64.4% | $0.0059 | 51.9 s |
| Haiku 4.5 | workflow | 53.6% | $0.0195 | 12.5 s |

So: **Jev is not the most accurate model on their own eval — it is the cheapest and fastest by 1–3
orders of magnitude, at mid-pack accuracy.** "Prompt mode" (no structured questions) scores much
lower for every model (e.g. Haiku: 53.6% → 18.1%), which is the shape of the argument they are making:
structure beats cleverness per dollar.

## 3. Why these numbers cannot be compared directly

1. **Different ground truth.** Their "accuracy" is *agreement with a frontier-model consensus*
   (average of Astra + Fable 5.1). Ours is *agreement with labels constructed by rule.* Frontier
   consensus is a much harder moving target: no model, including the reference models themselves
   scored against each other, would hit 100.
2. **Different task difficulty.** Their workflows involve 10+ question trees over realistic,
   multi-document state (alerts + tickets + device registries + schedules), and require selecting
   actions from large closed sets under code rules. Ours are 3-field decisions over single short
   contexts.
3. **Different metric shape.** They report per-workflow accuracy (likely per-action/question
   agreement, weighted across the workflow); we report per-case exact match — our strictest column.
4. **Different sample sizes.** Theirs is undisclosed; ours is n=24 (single deterministic run).
5. **The absurdity check.** If our 84.7% meant the same thing as their 67.8%, a 4-bit 8B on a laptop
   would be beating Opus 5 (73.1%) and Sol (74.1%) on their own eval. That is implausible. The
   parsimonious reading: **our metric is easier — not that we are better.**

## 4. What IS comparable (and favourable)

| Dimension | Jev | Local 8B | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Latency/case | 0.3–0.5 s | 0.65 s | **Same order of magnitude** |
| Cost/case | $0.0001–$0.0011 | ~$0 (electricity) | Local ~100–1000x cheaper |
| Schema/type safety | guaranteed by construction | guaranteed by construction | Tie |
| Accuracy | 61.7–76.0% vs frontier consensus | 84.7% vs constructed labels | **Not comparable** |
| Calibration | trained (they claim; no reliability plots published) | softmax proxy; measured weak error signal (7B: >0.90 confident on 13/20 wrong fields) | Jev likely ahead — untested |

The genuinely interesting, defensible claim after this comparison: **a free, local 8B with a
constraint-decoding harness delivers Jev-class latency and schema safety at ~0 marginal cost — but
its accuracy against a frontier-consensus metric is unknown.** That is the experiment to run next.

## 5. How to make it actually comparable (proposed experiment F)

TypeSafe's eval pages publish the workflow specs (question trees, thresholds, action sets) and
"View examples" links. A fair reproduction:

1. Pick the simplest workflow (Invoice Processing or the Security Incidents triage stage).
2. Rebuild the *same* questions as a schema for our engine; apply the *same* code rules.
3. Score against their *same* reference method: consensus of strong models (or, cheaper, against
   their published per-model answers if examples with labels are available).
4. Report agreement, not "accuracy" — and keep our per-question confidences visible to test their
   calibration claims on our side too.

Until that exists, any "we beat Jev" framing is unsupported; the numbers above are, deliberately,
kept next to each other without being equated.
